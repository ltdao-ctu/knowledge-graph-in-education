Application and effectiveness analysis
of AI Intelligent tutoring system combined
with knowledge graph in basic medical
pathology teaching

Yu Yuanyuan1 and Yuan Ying1*

Abstract
Background  Basic medical pathology teaching suffers from fragmented knowledge and insufficient personalized
learning, which impairs students’ systematic knowledge construction and learning efficiency. This study aimed to
apply an AI and knowledge graph integrated teaching model to address these challenges and provide references for
basic medical education reform.

Methods  An 18-week randomized controlled trial was conducted on 360 nursing students (180 each in
experimental/control groups). The control group used traditional teaching, while the experimental group adopted
the “AI + knowledge graph” integrated model with AI-assisted learning and knowledge graph reviews. SPSS was used
for quantitative and qualitative data analysis.

Results  The experimental group showed significantly better outcomes: final score (84.2 ± 6.9 vs. 74.1 ± 7.6), after
adjusting for class-level clustering using a two-level mixed-effects model, adjusted mean difference = 8.2, 95% CI:
4.7–11.7, P = 0.012, Cohen’s d = 1.13 (95% CI not computed), indicating a large effect size. knowledge mastery rate
(90.3% vs.75.8%), clinical case analysis score (28.5±3.2 vs. 21.3±4.1, P<0.001). It also reduced ineffective learning time.

Conclusion  This integrated model effectively promotes structured knowledge construction and learning efficiency,
realizing the synergistic effect of AI and knowledge graph. It provides a replicable and practical reference for the
digital and intelligent reform of basic medical education courses.

Keywords  AI Intelligent Tutoring System, Knowledge Graph, Pathology Teaching, Basic Medical Education,
Personalized Learning, Knowledge Construction

  Introduction
Currently,  global  medical  education  is  undergoing  a
profound  transformation  centered  on  digitalization  and
intelligence.  In  China,  this  process  has  received  policy
support.  The  “14th  Five-Year  Plan  for  Health  Human
Resources  Development”  explicitly  calls  for  promoting
the  deep  integration  of  emerging  technologies  such  as
artificial  intelligence  (AI)  and  virtual  reality  (VR)  with
medical  education,  aiming  to  build  a  personalized  and
efficient  teaching  model.  Against  this  backdrop,  pathol-
ogy,  a  core  discipline  bridging  basic  theory  and  clinical
practice  within  the  basic  medical  system,  has  become  a
key  focus  in  the  digital  transformation  of  medical  edu-
cation.  The  quality  of  pathology  instruction  directly
impacts  the  development  of  medical  students’  clinical
thinking and disease diagnostic capabilities.

The  knowledge  system  of  pathology  is  highly  com-
plex, primarily for three reasons. First, it covers a broad
range  of  topics,  encompassing  the  etiology,  pathological
changes, and mechanisms of hundreds of diseases across
various  human  organ  systems.  Second,  its  mechanisms
are abstract and challenging, requiring students to under-
stand  dynamic  processes  at  the  cellular  and  molecular
levels, such as the evolution of tumor atypia and inflam-
matory cascades. Third, the knowledge points are closely
interrelated,  with  complex  causal  chains,  as  exemplified
by  the  relationship  between  chronic  hepatitis,  cirrhosis,
and  hepatocellular  carcinoma  [1].  These  characteristics
pose  significant  challenges  to  the  traditional  teaching
model: students often perceive knowledge as fragmented
and  struggle  to  construct  a  systematic  cognitive  frame-
work,  while  instructors  find  it  difficult  to  provide  effec-
tive  guidance  tailored  to  individual  differences  due  to
rigid  teaching  schedules  [2].  Survey  data  indicate  that
nearly 40% of students report difficulties in understand-
ing,  memorizing,  and  applying  pathological  knowledge,
and  nearly  30%  of  teachers  believe  traditional  methods
fail to meet personalized needs, highlighting the urgency
of teaching reform.

To address these challenges, the academic community
has begun exploring technology-enabled approaches, the
development  of  which  can  be  divided  into  three  stages.
In the early stage (2009–2022), efforts focused primarily
on  the  internal  optimization  of  pedagogy  and  curricu-
lum  systems,  such  as  improving  teaching  effectiveness
through  curriculum  integration  or  research  mentorship
programs,  with  limited  deep  integration  of  digital  tech-
nologies  [3,  4].  During  the  intermediate  stage  (2023–
2024), with the rise of AI technology, pilot proposals and
exploratory  applications  of  single  technologies  emerged,
such  as  discussions  on  the  potential  of  AI  in  pathologi-
cal image recognition and case-based teaching. However,
these  often  lacked  systematic  implementation  frame-
works  and  structured  handling  of  knowledge  systems

[5–7].  Recently  (2025–present),  research  trends  have
shifted  toward  multi-technology  integration.  Scholars
have begun emphasizing the integration of medicine and
engineering and combining AI with knowledge visualiza-
tion technologies to address the issue of knowledge frag-
mentation.  However,  specific  collaborative  mechanisms
and  scalable  implementation  models  remain  underde-
veloped  [8].  Multimedia  technology  has  been  effectively
applied  in  the  teaching  of  pathological  cardiovascular
system,  verifying  the  value  of  technical  tools  in  improv-
ing the teaching effect of specific pathological subsystems
[9].

In summary, while existing research acknowledges the
value  of  digital  technologies,  limitations  persist,  includ-
ing  relatively  isolated  technology  applications,  insuffi-
cient  alignment  with  core  curriculum  needs,  and  a  lack
of  empirical  support  for  their  effectiveness.  In  terms  of
knowledge  graph  construction  technology,  the  accuracy
of  knowledge  entity  extraction  is  a  key  prerequisite  for
ensuring the quality of the graph [10]. The neural archi-
tectures for Named Entity Recognition proposed by Lam-
ple have been widely verified in medical text processing,
providing  a  mature  technical  path  for  the  extraction  of
pathological terms and knowledge points [11]. However,
few studies have applied this technology to the construc-
tion  of  pathology-specific  knowledge  graphs,  resulting
in  inconsistencies  in  the  definition  of  core  knowledge
entities  and  affecting  the  effectiveness  of  subsequent
knowledge  association  modeling.  Therefore,  explor-
ing  systematic  knowledge  extraction  and  representation
methods  is  essential  for  advancing  intelligent  pathology
education.  Knowledge  discovery  systems  provide  fun-
damental  support  for  identifying  implicit  and  explicit
knowledge from databases, laying a technical foundation
for knowledge structuring and intelligent tutoring appli-
cations [12].

The  proposal  for  this  research  topic  is  based  on  the
alignment  of  three  key  aspects.  First,  at  the  needs  level,
the  urgent  demands  of  pathology  education  for  knowl-
edge structuring and personalized learning closely match
the  adaptive  recommendation  capabilities  of  AI  systems
and the structured representation abilities of knowledge
graphs.  Second,  at  the  technology  level,  AI  algorithms
such  as  collaborative  filtering  and  deep  learning  can
already enable accurate analysis of learning behaviors and
content  delivery,  while  knowledge  graph  technologies
based  on  RDF  and  graph  databases  can  efficiently  con-
struct and maintain complex networks of medical knowl-
edge  associations. This  combination  of  technologies  has
been empirically validated in the field of medical educa-
tion [13]. Finally, cross-domain experience offers valuable
insights:  incremental  update  algorithms  for  knowledge
graphs  from  computer  science  can  address  the  rapid
evolution  of  medical  knowledge  [14];  The  Kirkpatrick

build  a  multidimensional  assessment  system  beyond
exam  scores;  and  Self-Determination  Theory  from  psy-

In  light  of  this,  the  present  study  aims  to  develop  and
validate  a  teaching  model  that  deeply  integrates  an  AI
Intelligent  Tutoring  System  with  a  Knowledge  Graph.
This  model  is  not  merely  a  superficial  combination  of
technologies;  rather,  it  employs  the  knowledge  graph
as  a  structured  knowledge  engine  and  the  AI  system  as
a  personalized  learning  agent.  It  seeks  to  fundamentally
address  the  core  challenges  in  pathology  education—
often  characterized  by  high  volume,  complexity,  and
learning  difficulties—thereby  providing  an  innovative
and  practical  exemplar  for  the  digital  reform  of  medical
education.

Although  this  study  focuses  on  pathology,  the  pro-
posed  bidirectional  AI–knowledge  graph  integration
framework  is  designed  to  be  adaptable  to  other  medical
courses.  The  generalizability  and  implementation  chal-
lenges across different knowledge domains warrant sepa-
rate investigation.

Selection and grouping of research subjects

A total of 401 first-year nursing students from eight nat-
ural  classes  at  a  medical  college  were  initially  enrolled.
Later,  41  students  transferred  to  other  majors,  resulting
in  360  eligible  participants.  To  ensure  homogeneity  and
study validity, the following inclusion and exclusion crite-
ria were established. The participant flow diagram is pre-
sented in Fig. 1.

Inclusion criteria:

  – Enrolled in the 2024 nursing program at the college,
having completed prerequisite courses (Human
Anatomy and Physiology) with passing grades;
  – No prior learning or training experience in basic

pathological sciences;

  – Voluntary participation and signed written informed

consent;

  – Possessing basic operational skills with electronic
devices (smartphone or computer) to properly use
the AI-powered intelligent tutoring system and the
knowledge graph platform.

Exclusion criteria:

  – Failure to complete or pass the prerequisite courses;
  – Prior exposure to pathology content or participation
in other pathology teaching reform experiments
during the study period;

  – Inability to complete the 18-week teaching

experiment due to academic leave, major transfer, or
clinical internship;

  – Lack of basic electronic device operation skills,

making it impossible to use the study’s supporting
teaching tools.

A  cluster  randomized  controlled  design  was  adopted,
with  randomization  performed  at  the  class  level.  Using
SPSS 26.0 to generate random numbers, the eight classes
were allocated to the experimental group (4 classes, 180
students) and the control group (4 classes, 180 students).
Independent-samples  t-tests  showed  no  statistically  sig-
nificant differences in key baseline variables between the
two groups (P > 0.05), and no significant differences were
found  among  classes  within  each  group  (all  P  >  0.05).
Baseline  characteristics  were  as  follows:  sex  distribu-
tion (experimental group: 78 males, 102 females; control
group: 75 males, 105 females), mean prerequisite course
score (experimental group: 71.2 ± 6.8; control group: 70.8

AI–knowledge graph integrated teaching system
architecture

This  system  is  driven  by  a  dual-core  mechanism  com-
bining  a  knowledge  graph  and  an  AI  teaching  assistant,
forming  a  closed-loop  intelligent  teaching  architecture
characterized by “knowledge guiding learning and learn-
ing feeding back to knowledge.”

The  knowledge  graph  organizes  pathology  knowledge
into  a  visualized,  structured  relational  system,  compris-
ing  89  core  knowledge  points,  356  weighted  relations,
and  hierarchical  semantic  associations.  The  graph  is
constructed based on the 10th edition of Pathology pub-
lished  by  People’s  Medical  Publishing  House,  following
a  dual-track  approach  of  “top-down  framework  defini-
tion and bottom-up detail supplementation.” It has been
reviewed  and  validated  by  five  senior  pathology  profes-
sors to ensure professional rigor.

The  AI  teaching  assistant  module  analyzes  students’
learning  status  and  provides  personalized  recommen-
dations.  It  comprises  three  intelligent  components:  a
knowledge-aware recommendation module, a pathology-
adapted BERT QA module, and a dynamic path planning
module [18, 19].

The  knowledge  graph  supplies  relation  weights  and
semantic constraints to the AI, while the AI, in turn, feeds
back  students’  high-frequency  errors  and  concentrated

queries  to  the  graph,  dynamically  adjusting  the  relation
weights among knowledge points. This enables continu-
ous self-optimization of knowledge priorities.

All  performance  metrics  reported  above  are  based
on  internal  tests  using  a  separate  cohort  (2023  nursing
students,  non-overlapping  with  the  RCT  sample).  No
independent  external  validation  has  been  performed.
Although  the  test  data  are  independent  of  the  RCT
cohort  (avoiding  direct  circularity),  the  benchmarks
remain internal, non-blinded, and without pre-registered
thresholds. Optimistic bias is possible; external validation
is needed.

The  experimental  cycle  was  1  semester  (18  weeks).  The
teaching  resources  of  the  two  groups  were  strictly  con-
sistent (the same teaching team, the same theoretical and
experimental  teaching  content,  and  unified  assessment
standards and scoring rules), with only differences in the
teaching models. The specific implementation and qual-
ity control measures are as follows:

To avoid the impact of differences in teaching operation

multiple  quality  control  mechanisms  were  set  up:  Two
full-time  research  assistants  formed  a  monitoring  team.
They  weekly  exported  system  usage  logs  of  the  experi-

check compliance with the minimum effective operation
requirement:  ≥90%  of  students  were  required  to  com-

with  qualified  effective  duration.  At  the  same  time,  the
completion  rate  of  group  tasks  in  the  knowledge  graph
review sessions was recorded on-site to ensure adherence
to the standard process of path drawing and discussion.

For students who failed to meet the standards for two
consecutive  weeks,  research  assistants  conducted  one-
on-one meetings with course teachers to analyze reasons
for  non-compliance  (e.g.,  unskilled  operation,  time  con-
flicts), provide system usage guidance, or adjust learning
plans.  For  groups  with  low  participation  in  knowledge
graph  tasks,  teachers  increased  the  frequency  of  guid-
ance in subsequent courses to ensure the effective imple-
mentation of teaching intervention measures [21].

The  basic  data  of  the  two  groups,  such  as  classroom
attendance  and  homework  submission  rates,  were  com-
pared monthly to ensure no significant differences. After
the experiment, third-party statisticians conducted cross-
validation of the teaching process data and score data of
the  two  groups  to  exclude  the  impact  of  data  recording
errors on the result analysis.

Data collection and analysis

Quantitative  data  were  collected  using  a  scoring  system
combined with an AI backend platform, including base-
line  prerequisite  course  scores  (Human  Anatomy  and
Physiology,  collected  in  Week  1  as  a  proxy  for  general
academic ability, because students had no prior exposure
to pathology), mid-term exam scores (Week 9), and final
exam scores (Week 18) for both groups. Additional quan-
titative indicators for the experimental group comprised
knowledge point mastery rate (calculated using Formula
1), learning time distribution, and the proportion of dif-
ferent question types in Q&A interactions.

Knowledge mastery rate

For  each  core  knowledge  point,  the  AI  tutoring  system
automatically administered 5–10 quiz questions after the
student  completed  the  corresponding  learning  module.
The mastery rate for a given knowledge point was calcu-
lated using the following formula:

100%

where  C  represents  the  number  of  correct  student
responses and T is the total number of questions pertain-
ing to the knowledge point. Mastery rates were updated
daily  based  on  the  students’  most  recent  responses.  All
data were recorded in the backend of the AI system.

Learning duration

For  the  experimental  group,  the  AI  system  automati-
cally recorded the total time spent on the platform each
day,  including  time  on  micro-lecture  videos,  exercises
and  knowledge  graph  viewing.  Effective  learning  time
was defined as active engagement (e.g., answering ques-
tions, watching videos, navigating the knowledge graph),
excluding  idle  time  (no  mouse  or  keyboard  activity  for
more  than  30  s)  and  repetition  of  content  already  mas-
tered.  For  the  control  group,  daily  study  time  was  col-
lected  through  class  attendance  records  and  brief
monthly  surveys.  Both  groups’  duration  data  were  aver-
aged over the 18 weeks period.

Upon completion of the experiment, two surveys were
administered  to  the  experimental  group.  The  first  was
a  5-point  Likert  scale  questionnaire  consisting  of  20
items  across  dimensions  including  system  usability  and
the  perceived  helpfulness  of  the  knowledge  graph.  This
questionnaire  was  a  brief,  unvalidated  satisfaction  sur-
vey  developed  specifically  for  this  study,  without  formal
validity verification, reliability testing (Cronbach’s α), fac-
tor analysis, or test-retest reliability. A total of 180 ques-
tionnaires  were  distributed,  with  176  valid  responses
recovered.  The  second  was  a  focus  group  interview,  for
which 12 students were selected representing three per-
formance levels: high, average, and underachieving. Each
interview lasted approximately 30 min to document par-
ticipants’ usage experiences and suggestions for improve-
ment. The focus group interview protocol was developed
specifically  for  this  study,  and  its  full  English  version  is
included as Supplementary Material. Qualitative analysis
of the focus group transcripts followed thematic analysis
procedures.  Two  researchers  independently  coded  the
first three transcripts using open coding, compared their
coding schemes, and resolved disagreements through dis-
cussion to form an initial codebook. The remaining tran-
scripts were coded independently by the two researchers,
inter-coder  agreement  of  87%  (disagreements

This  study  adopted  a  cluster  randomized  design  with
classes as  clustering units, leading  to non-independence
of  student  observations.  The  primary  outcome  (final
exam score) was analyzed using a two-level mixed-effects
model: level 1 represented individual students, and level
2 included random intercepts for classes; the fixed effect
was  intervention  group  assignment.  The  model  was
implemented  in  SPSS  26.0  using  the  MIXED  procedure
with restricted maximum likelihood (REML) estimation.
The  intraclass  correlation  coefficient  (ICC)  was  calcu-
lated  from  the  null  model  (without  covariates)  to  assess
the  magnitude  of  clustering.  For  sensitivity  analysis,
independent  samples  t-test  ignoring  clustering  was  also
performed.  A  P-value < 0.05  was  considered  statistically
significant.

Results
Teaching process data

The experimental group had an average of 2.6 daily logins
and  an  average  daily  learning  duration  of  43.8 ± 6.5  min
(including  42.3%  for  video  learning,  35.1%  for  exer-
cise  practice,  and  22.6%  for  knowledge  graph  view-
ing);  the  high-frequency  access  knowledge  points  of  the
knowledge  graph  were  pathological  characteristics  of
tumors(accounting for 31.2% of accesses) and pathogen-
esis  of  cardiovascular  diseases  (accounting  for  25.7%  of
accesses),  reflecting  students’  focus  on  complex  knowl-
edge  points.  During  the  experiment,  the  AI  system
answered a total of 6,842 questions for the experimental
group, with the distribution of question types as follows:
mechanism-related  (e.g.,  differences  between  necrosis
and apoptosis mechanisms) accounting for 48.5%, differ-
entiation-related (e.g., pathological differentiation of var-
ious  types  of  glomerulonephritis)  accounting  for  32.7%,
and clinical association-related (e.g., association between
pathological  results  and  clinical  symptoms)  accounting
for 18.8%, indicating that students pay more attention to

in-depth understanding needs of knowing not only what

Learning behavior dimension: transformation from blind
learning to accurate and efficient learning

The average daily effective learning duration of the exper-
imental group increased by 20% compared to the control
group, but the proportion of repeated question brushing
decreased  by  16.8%.  The  core  reason  lies  in  the  person-

based  on  the  knowledge  point  mastery  rate  calculation
formula,  the  system  can  accurately  identify  students’
knowledge  blind  spots  (e.g.,  a  student’s  mastery  rate  of
pathological types of glomerulonephritis is only 52%) and
prioritize  pushing  targeted  learning  resources,  avoiding
the  blindness  of  brushing  questions  for  the  entire  chap-

path  query  function  of  the  knowledge  graph  signifi-
cantly improved the cross-chapter knowledge integration
efficiency  of  students  in  the  experimental  group.  For
example,  when  learning  about  gastric  cancer,  students
can  quickly  retrieve  the  pathological  development  path
of  chronic  gastritis  →  intestinal  metaplasia  →  gastric
cancer  and  core  mechanisms,  reducing  the  time  cost  of
fragmented memory of knowledge points. This is mutu-
ally confirmed by the high recognition rate of improved
learning  efficiency  (91.5%)  in  the  questionnaire  survey
[26](Fig. 3).

Research shortcomings and future improvement directions

The  present  study  has  several  limitations  that  should  be
addressed in future research.

First,  sample  homogeneity  and  the  limited  number  of
clusters. All participants were nursing students recruited
from  a  single  medical  college  in  Southwest  China.  This
highly homogeneous sample limits the ability to capture
differences in learning contexts across institutions of dif-
ferent  tiers,  academic  disciplines,  or  teaching  resources,
thereby restricting the generalizability and external valid-
ity  of  the  findings.  Furthermore,  this  study  employed  a
cluster-randomized controlled trial design with only four
clusters  (classes)  per  group,  resulting  in  a  limited  num-
ber of clusters. Although a multilevel model was used to
adjust for the clustering effect at the class level, the small
number of clusters may still compromise the stability of
variance estimates. Therefore, future studies should con-
duct multi-center, large-scale cluster-randomized trials—
for example, by including two or three higher vocational
medical colleges at different tiers and one undergraduate
institution,  with  a  total  sample  size  of  no  less  than  800
students—to validate the robustness of the results [31].

Second,  systematic  optimization  for  diverse  educa-
tional  settings.  To  enable  large-scale  application  of  the
system,  the  association  weights  in  the  knowledge  graph
and AI recommendation algorithm need to be tailored to
the  teaching  objectives  of  each  institution.  For  example,
in  higher  vocational  education,  the  association  between
pathological manifestations and operational skills should
be  strengthened,  whereas
in  undergraduate  educa-
tion,  the  linkage  between  pathological  mechanisms  and
research advances should be deepened. Such adaptations
would  allow  the  construction  of  a  stratified,  adaptive
teaching system capable of meeting the needs of different
medical education scenarios.

Third,  transferability  potential  and  challenges  to  other
basic medical courses. Although the current system was
validated  in  the  context  of  pathology,  its  architecture  is
domain-agnostic  and  can  theoretically  be  transferred  to
other  courses  [32].  For  instance,  in  pharmacology,  the
knowledge  graph  could  be  structured  to  build  a  pathol-
ogy–disease–drug–therapeutic
target  relational  net-
work, facilitating understanding of drug mechanisms and
adverse  reaction  pathways.  However,  such  transfer  may
face  two  major  challenges:  first,  the  high  cost  of  knowl-
edge  engineering,  which  requires  deep  involvement  of
domain  experts  and  the  establishment  of  a  standard-
ized  ontology;  second,  the  difficulty  of  course  integra-
tion,  given  the  substantial  differences  across  courses  in
knowledge abstraction and the degree of clinical integra-
tion, which necessitates the design of flexible association
weighting strategies.

Finally,  we  will  develop  and  validate  a  standardized,
psychometrically sound scale in future research to rigor-
ously  assess  students’  learning  experiences  and  behav-
ioral  changes  in  AI-  and  knowledge  graph-assisted
teaching.

Potential biases

Teacher  effect  bias.  Teachers  were  not  blinded  to  group
allocation,  which  may  have  introduced  unconscious
expectancy  effects  (e.g.,  differential  attention  or  encour-
agement). Future studies should blind teachers or record
interaction  patterns  to  eliminate  differences  in  teacher
behavior or quantify the impact they may have [33].

Novelty effect. The experimental group’s first exposure
to  the  AI-KG  system  might  have  temporarily  boosted
engagement due to novelty rather than the intervention’s
inherent effectiveness. The 18-week duration partly miti-
gates this, but crossover designs or longer follow-ups are
needed to separate novelty from true effects.

Hawthorne  effect.  The  discrepancy  in  process  moni-
toring  intensity  between  the  experimental  and  control
groups  may  introduce  Hawthorne  effects  and  attention
bias.  In  future  studies,  we  will  adopt  an  equal-intensity
monitoring design or covert observation methods to mit-
igate the interference of such biases on the results.

Experimenter  bias.  The  researchers  were  not  blinded
to group assignment and were involved in delivering the
intervention  and  collecting  outcome  data,  which  may
have  unconsciously  influenced  student  performance  or
data  recording.  Future  studies  should  use  blinded  out-
come assessors and, where possible, automated data col-
lection to minimise this bias [34].

In summary, these biases may have led to overestima-
tion  of  the  intervention  effect.  Therefore,  the  findings
should  be  interpreted  with  caution,  and  future  studies
are encouraged to employ more rigorous and appropriate
methodologies  to  minimize  the  impact  of  these  limita-
tions on the results.

Absence  of  a  pathology-specific  pre-test.  Because  stu-
dents had no prior exposure to pathology, a conventional
pre-test on pathological content was not feasible. There-
fore, we used prerequisite course scores to establish base-
line equivalence in general learning ability, and evaluated
the  intervention  effect  solely  by  post-test  comparison
between groups.

Conclusions
Main findings summary

This study conducted an 18-week randomized controlled
trial involving 360 nursing students in Southwest China.
It explored the application effect of an AI intelligent tutor-
ing  system  integrated  with  knowledge  graphs  in  basic
medical pathology teaching. The results showed that this
integrated  teaching  model  had  significant  effects.  Com-
pared  with  traditional  teaching,  the  experimental  group
achieved a final mean score of 84.2 ± 6.9, which was sig-
nificantly higher than the control group’s 74.1 ± 7.6. After
adjusting  for  class-level  clustering  effects,  the  mean  dif-
ference was 8.2 points (95% CI: 4.7–11.7, P = 0.012), cor-
responding to an 11.1% relative improvement. The overall

knowledge  mastery  rate  reached  90.3%,  an  increase  of
14.5%  points.  The  experimental  group  exhibited  a  more
significant improvement in accuracy for questions about
complex pathological mechanisms and clinical case anal-
yses.  This  indicated  that  the  integrated  model  not  only
enhanced  students’  memory  and  mastery  of  basic  path-
ological  knowledge  but  also  effectively  improved  their
ability  to  apply  theoretical  knowledge  to  practical  prob-
lems and develop clinical associative thinking.

Furthermore,  the  structured  association  characteristic
of  pathological  knowledge  graphs  helped  alleviate  the
issue  of  fragmented  knowledge  acquisition  among  stu-
dents. The personalized recommendation function of the
AI tutoring system mitigated the challenges of unfocused
learning  and  inefficient  review.  Together,  they  formed
a  relatively  complete  closed-loop  intelligent  teaching
framework. Among the experimental group, the propor-
tion of effective learning time increased by 20% and the
proportion of ineffective practice questions decreased by
16.8%.  These  results  demonstrate  the  favorable  perfor-
mance of the integrated teaching model incorporating AI
and knowledge graphs.

Nevertheless, due to the limited number of clusters (4
per group), the precision of the adjusted effect estimate is
uncertain. Larger multi-cluster trials are needed to con-
firm these preliminary findings.

Practical implications

From  the  perspective  of  medical  education  practice,  the
research has important practical guiding significance for
promoting the reform and optimization of basic medical
pathology teaching. Firstly, it breaks through the limita-
tions  of  traditional  pathology  teaching  such  as  teacher-
centered  indoctrination  and  separation  of  theory  and
practice, provides a new feasible path for the digital and
intelligent  transformation  of  pathology  teaching,  and
enriches the forms of intelligent teaching in basic medi-
cal  education.  Secondly,  the  integrated  teaching  model
effectively  solves  the  key  difficulties  in  pathology  teach-
ing,  such  as  abstract  and  complex  knowledge,  difficult
logical  association,  and  large  individual  differences  in
students’ learning, helps students construct a systematic
and structured pathological knowledge system, improves
their  learning  efficiency  and  comprehensive  quality,  and
provides  effective  support  for  cultivating  high-quality
nursing  professionals  with  solid  professional  foundation
and strong practical ability. Thirdly, the research verifies
the feasibility and effectiveness of the combination of AI
technology and knowledge graph in pathology teaching,
provides  practical  reference  for  the  popularization  and
application of similar intelligent teaching models in other
basic medical courses (such as anatomy, physiology), and
has important promotion value for promoting the high-
quality development of basic medical education.

Future studies should validate the “AI + knowledge graph”
teaching  model  across  different  grade  levels,  academic
majors  (e.g.,  clinical  medicine,  medical  laboratory  sci-
ence), and types of medical institutions (e.g., undergradu-
ate  universities,  vocational  colleges)  to  further  enhance
the generalizability and adaptability of the model.

Optimize the functionality and performance of the teaching
system

First, the dynamic updating mechanism of the pathology
knowledge graph should be further refined by enriching
the  types  of  knowledge  associations  and  expanding  the
repository  of  clinical  case  resources,  thereby  improving
alignment with the latest textbooks and clinical practice.
Second,  the  AI-driven  recommendation  algorithm  and
personalized learning path planning should be optimized
by integrating a broader range of multidimensional learn-
ing  behavior  data,  thereby  enhancing  the  accuracy  and
relevance of intelligent tutoring. Third, longitudinal stud-
ies should be conducted to track the impact of the teach-
ing  model  on  students’  subsequent  academic  learning
and  professional  career  development,  providing  deeper
evidence for the long-term value of this model.

Strengthen the integration of the “AI + knowledge graph”
teaching model with offline classroom instruction

A  blended  teaching  model  combining  online  intelligent
tutoring  with  offline  interactive  classroom  activities
should  be  developed.  This  approach  will  further  lever-
age the advantages of intelligent technologies to promote
comprehensive improvements in the quality of pathology
education.

Transfer the validated “AI + knowledge graph” integration
framework to other basic medical science courses

For  example:  In  pharmacology,  the  framework  could
highlight  the  logical  chain  of  drug  component–patho-
logical  mechanism–disease  treatment.  Specific  nodes
for drug targets and dynamic associations between drug
efficacy  and  pathological  changes  could  be  added.  In
the  recommendation  module,  priority  should  be  given
to matching drug mechanisms of action with pathologi-
cal changes, while the question-answering module could
be  enhanced  to  explain  the  pathological  mechanisms
underlying  adverse  drug  reactions.  In  physiology,  the
core framework could be structured around physiological
function–organ  structure–regulatory  mechanism,  with
additional  links  connecting  physiological  abnormalities

to  pathological  precursors  and  layered  representations
of  complex  physiological  regulatory  networks.  The  rec-
ommendation  module  could  deliver  animated  demon-
strations  and  simulated  experiment  resources  tailored
to  students’  weaknesses  in  understanding  physiological
mechanisms.
